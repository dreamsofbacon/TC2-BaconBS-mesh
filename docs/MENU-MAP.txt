Bacon BBS - menu map
====================

Every path from the main menu. Stops at each game's own launch item --
what happens inside Zork, Trivia King, etc. is that game's own interface,
not this BBS's menu tree.

Numbers and letters here are the canonical set from example_config.ini. A
given node's config.ini may configure a smaller subset (main_menu_items,
bbs_menu_items), with required entries (Ask Nomad, Services, Settings &
Profile, Node View) inserted automatically if left out -- so the NUMBERS
below can shift on a node with a trimmed list, while the letters cannot.

Verified against a live node by walking it over SSH, 2026-09-20 (v0.1.688).
Where an earlier version behaved differently, the change is noted.

Getting around
--------------
  !<letter>   reaches any main-menu item from anywhere: !Q !B !G !H !N !A
              !S !V !P !X. Works inside every menu, including the Games
              menu and Public Chatter (before v0.1.688 those two rejected
              it). An open door game is the exception -- inside Zork or
              Trivia King, "!" and "?" are the game's input.
  ?           shows the main menu, from anywhere except inside a door game
              and except at a prompt that is collecting text (a subject
              line of "?" is a subject). Added in v0.1.688; before that the
              new-account welcome advertised it and nothing implemented it.
  !cancel     backs out of any in-progress form. !exit, !x and !0 are the
              same thing.
  [0]         goes back one level. At the MAIN menu it disconnects, and
              that is the only place it does.

  The letter X is never printed: build_menu renders it as "[0]". Typing a
  bare "x" is refused like any other key that is not on screen (before
  v0.1.688 it silently disconnected you).

MAIN MENU   header: Bacon BBS  (mail:N shows unread count)
------------------------------------------------------------
[1] Quick Commands
    -- a reference card, not a menu. Lists !SM !CM !R !AU !PB !CB !CHP
       !CHL !VER !WELCOME and the !<letter> menu jumps.

[2] BBS
    [1] Mail
        [1] Read
            Empty: "There are no messages in your mailbox."
            Otherwise a numbered list; reply with a number to read one,
            D to delete several, or 0 to go back.
              -> a message, then [1]Keep [2]Delete [3]Reply
              -> [2] Delete asks "Delete ...? [Y]es [0]No" first
              -> D takes "3,5", "3-7" or ALL, lists what it will delete,
                 and confirms before doing it
        [2] Send
            A picker of people who have switched ON Offline mail relay,
            plus [A]ddress to type a name, and [N]ext/[P]revious when the
            list runs to more than one page.
            NOTE: mail only reaches someone who has turned relay on in
            THEIR OWN Settings > [3]. There is no way to write to an
            account that has not. Two brand-new accounts cannot mail each
            other until one of them opts in.
              -> recipient -> subject -> body (END to send)
        [3] Relay Directory
            The same list, to browse rather than to pick. A number writes
            to that person.
        [0] Back -> BBS Menu
    [2] Bulletins
        Board picker: General / Info / News / Urgent, per [boards] config.
        Accepts the number, the board name, or its first letter.
        A board marked * does not sync everywhere: its posts stay on this
        node, or reach only the nodes the operator chose. The legend says
        so on the picker, because that is where people decide what to
        write.
        [S]ync setup appears for ADMINS only -> pick a board -> [1]
        Everywhere [2] This node only [3] Chosen nodes (toggle peers by
        number, [D]one). It applies to posts written from then on; posts
        already sent stay where they are. Same setting as the web admin's
        Settings > Board Sync panel.
          -> [1]Read [2]Post [0]Back
             [1] Read  -> "Select a bulletin number to view from <board>,
                           or [0] to go back:"
                          -> the post, and you stay on the list: reply
                             with another number to read the next one.
                          -> [E]dit and [D]elete appear on a post YOU
                             wrote. A moderator gets delete on any post.
                             Deletes travel to every node as tombstones.
                          -> [E]dit collects the replacement text (END to
                             finish) and rewrites the body; the subject,
                             board, date and author stay as they were, and
                             the web admin edits those. The post is
                             retracted and republished under a new id,
                             because peers reconcile by id and would push
                             their copy of the original back otherwise.
                             Only on the node that wrote the post, and
                             editing never widens where it may travel.
             [2] Post  -> subject -> body (END to post)
                          The Urgent board is radio-only on most nodes:
                          an SSH poster is refused and told why.
    [3] Channel Dir
        [1]View [2]Post [0]Back
          -> View lists channels, opens one, reads and adds comments
          -> Post starts a new channel (name, then link/PSK)
    [4] JS8CALL   -- only when a JS8Call link is configured on the node
    [0] Back -> MAIN

[3] Games
    Titles are listed a group at a time. A group holding one title shows
    the title itself, and opens it in one key.
    [1] Adventures           -> [1] Zork I      [6] Enchanter
                                [2] Zork II     [7] Planetfall
                                [3] Zork III    [8] Starcross
                                [4] Hitchhiker's Guide
                                [5] Deadline    [9] Baconfall: The Last Sizzle
                                [0] Back -> Games
    [2] Classics             -> [1] Candy Wars  (Dope Wars in PG-13 mode)
                                [2] Hamurabi          [5] Lemonade Stand
                                [3] Hunt the Wumpus   [6] Oregon Trail
                                [4] Lunar Lander
                                [0] Back -> Games
    [3] Daily                -> [1] Word of the Day
                                [2] Number of the Day
                                [3] Skillet Keep
                                [0] Back -> Games
    [4] Trivia King
    [S]cores [H]all of Fame [F]ortune [0]Back
      [S] the same groups, then a title -> that game's high scores
      [H] one line per title, "-" where nobody has played
      [F] a fortune, then back to the Games menu
    Inside a game, X leaves without losing your place.
    An operator can hide titles (Settings > Games). The lists renumber, and
    a group with nothing left in it is not shown.

    The first time a player opens any of these games -- the classics,
    the daily puzzles and Skillet Keep -- its rules come first, as their
    own message, then the game. After that it opens straight on the game;
    [?] or HELP sends the rules again, then the screen.

    The classics are played by numbered menu. On a screen that asks for an
    amount, 0 is an amount (no food, no land), so [B] goes back there;
    everywhere else [0] goes back, or saves and exits from the game's main
    screen. X leaves from anywhere.

    Hamurabi -- rule a city for ten years. Each year opens with a plan that
        feeds everyone and plants what the grain allows:
        [1]Land [2]Feed [3]Plant [4]End year [?]Help [0]Exit
        [1]-[3] each explain themselves and ask one number (a minus sells
        land). The plan line shows the grain left, or how much it is short.
        A finished reign is scored; being deposed is not.
    Hunt the Wumpus -- twenty rooms, three tunnels each.
        [1]-[3]Go to <room> [4]Shoot [?]Help [0]Exit
        [4] aims the arrow a room at a time, up to five, never back the way
        it came: pick the first room, then [1]Loose it or [2]-[3]On to
        <room>; [0] takes back the last room. Only a kill scores.
    Lunar Lander -- a burn of 0-40 each five seconds, or several in one
        message: 20 20 16. 0 is a burn here, so [X] is the way out. A
        landing scores; a crash does not.
    Lemonade Stand -- ten days. Each day's plan carries over from the last:
        [1]Glasses [2]Signs [3]Price [4]Open stand [?]Help [0]Exit
        [1]-[3] each explain themselves and ask one number. The score is
        the cash you finish with.
    Oregon Trail -- 2040 miles in 18 turns.
        General store: [1]Oxen [2]Food [3]Ammo [4]Clothes [5]Supplies
            (each asks one dollar amount and says what it buys)
            [6]Ready-made outfit [7]Set out [?]Help [0]Exit
        On the trail: [1]Travel on [2]Hunt [3]Rations [4]Fort [?]Help [0]Exit
            [3] -> [1]Poorly [2]Moderately [3]Well; the choice sticks
            [4] only where there is a fort: [1]Food [2]Ammo [3]Clothes
            [4]Supplies [5]Travel on [0]Back. Prices are half again home's,
            and a stop costs 45 miles on the next leg.
        [?] sends the rules, then help for the screen you are on. Arriving
        scores.
    In all five, every message is one packet and leaving keeps your place.

    Every game's save is kept in one place, so where [sync]
    sync_zork_saves is on a game follows its player between nodes. Nothing is sent per turn: the sync
    cycle fetches a save that has changed, and the newer of two copies wins
    -- so playing the same game on two nodes before they have synced keeps
    only the later session.

    Daily -- one puzzle a day, the same on every node (worked out from the
    UTC date, so nothing is synced), and one go each. Solving on
    consecutive days builds a streak, which is what the scoreboard ranks.
    Word of the Day -- a 5-letter word in 6 guesses. Each guess comes back
        marked: CAPITAL right place, small letter elsewhere in the word,
        dot not in it.   CRANE  C . a . E
    Number of the Day -- 4 different digits in 8 guesses. B = right digit,
        right place; C = right digit, wrong place.   1325 1B 2C

    Skillet Keep -- a daily-turn adventure in the Baconfall world. Twelve
    turns a day; the day turns at midnight UTC and a night's rest heals.
        [1] Wilds   [1]Near [2]Deep [3]Far -- one turn, one whole fight,
                    reported in a sentence. [0] back to the keep.
        [2] Smith   [1] the next blade [2] the next armor
        [3] Inn     heal fully, for gold
        [4] Bank    [1]Deposit all [2]Withdraw all -- banked gold is safe
        [5] Warden  needs this level's xp; beating one is a level. The
                    tenth is the Ash King: a crown, and a fresh start.
        [6] Board   who is in the keep, strongest first, and the news
        [7] Duel    once a day, against the nearest-level player as they
                    stand; their save is not touched
        [?] rules   [X] save and exit ([0] goes back a screen here)
    Falling in a fight costs half the gold you carry and ends your day.
    The scoreboard ranks lifetime experience, and is updated on a level or
    a crown rather than after every fight, to keep it off the air.

    Candy Wars -- buy low, sell high, 30 or 365 days. Played by menu:
        [1]Market [2]Travel [3]Bag [4]Gear [5]Sibling [6]End [0]Exit
        Market lists what is for sale here: "$price/stock", and "+n" for
        what is already in your bag. Sixteen goods do not fit one packet,
        so [M] pages, and the footer says which page of how many; the
        numbers belong to the goods, not the page, so [5] works even on a
        page it is not printed on. Picking one offers [1]Buy [2]Sell.
        A reply is read a word at a time, so "1 5 1 5" is Market -> the
        fifth good -> Buy -> five of them, and "1 5 1 M" buys as many as
        you can. T travels, from here or from the main screen, and
        arriving somewhere shows you what it sells.
        [5]Money is the loan and the bank together. The bank is in one
        town -- the Cafeteria, or Brooklyn in PG-13 mode -- and what is
        in it is out of reach of a Hall Monitor fine, though not of the
        loan deadline. A balance shows on the main screen as "saved $n".
        The Hall Monitor sometimes stops you: [1]Talk your way out
        [2]Run for it [3]Hand over your candy.
        In PG-13 mode the same run reads as Dope Wars -- only the words
        change, never the save.

[4] Public Chatter
    Window picker: [1]1h [2]3h [3]6h [4]24h [5]3d [6]7d
      -> the feed for that window, then [F]ilter [T]ime [0]Back
         [F] filter by channel
         [T] back to the window picker
    Radio traffic the node overheard, not BBS posts.

[5] Ask Nomad
    A question prompt. The answer arrives asynchronously -- it can land on
    whatever screen you have moved to since. Afterwards you are offered
    another question or the main menu.
    Each question is independent: no context is carried between them.
    If the node's AI server has no usable model, Ask Nomad says so at the
    door rather than taking the question first.

[6] Services
    [1] Weather & Safety   [1]Weather now [2]3-day forecast [3]NWS alerts
                           [4]Recent quakes [5]Sunrise & sunset [6]Tides
    [2] Propagation & Sky  [1]Solar & band conditions [2]Geomagnetic Kp
                           [3]Space weather outlook [4]ISS position
    [3] Reference          [1]Wikipedia [2]Dictionary [3]Callsign lookup
                           [4]Exchange rates [5]Time by zone
    [4] News               [1]Hacker News, plus any news feeds the fleet
                           has added. Once feeds are filed into more than
                           one category, News grows a category screen
                           between here and the feeds.
    Doors that need one ask for an argument (a ZIP, an article title, a
    callsign). The answer arrives with an invitation to pick another from
    the same list, or [0] to go back.

[7] Settings & Profile      (!P and !S both reach this)
    The card at the top is who you are: name, the alias your posts appear
    under when it differs, how long you have been here, and how much you
    have written.
    [1] Edit bio
    [2] Linked devices
        [1]..[5] the usual link/unlink actions
        [6] ...
        [7] Reset SSH password  -- sends a six-digit code to a linked
            radio; log in as reset:<alias> with it to set a new one
    [3] Offline mail relay   -- ON means other people can send you mail
        and the BBS pushes it to your radio. Confirms before changing.
    [4] Node View
    [5] Help tips            -- the "Tip:" lines under each menu
    [6] About this node
    [7] View Stats           [1]Nodes [2]Hardware [3]Roles
    [8] PG-13 mode           off by default; on shows games' mature
                             versions. Follows your account to other
                             nodes. "(set by this node)" means the
                             operator has locked it for everyone.

[8] Node View
    Which nodes' content this session shows. Lenses and filters.

[0] Exit
    Disconnects. "73!"
